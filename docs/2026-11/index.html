Research

A thin month. Three PubMed queries by entry date for Oct 1–31 returned 25 unique records; I read all the abstracts and two were usable for this audience. Both are summarized from abstracts only. Papers already carded in No. 0 (Delabar, Corsi) are not repeated.

Pilot methods comparisonn = 7Service-dog training, one litterVet. Anim. Sci. · Oct 14Abstract only

Facial-behavior, cortisol, and observer welfare measures didn't line up in seven training dogs

Cherry et al. compared three welfare measures in the same dogs. Per the abstract, facial-behavior rankings showed no agreement with cortisol, while expert assessments agreed most strongly with urinary cortisol.

“we compared three such measures in seven purpose-bred Labrador Retrievers from a single litter during standardized early-stage training”

Seven related dogs, no non-training control, and service-dog training rather than a shelter. The authors write: “Because this was a small, closely related cohort assessed without a non-training control condition, these results cannot establish the validity of any measure.” The record states no known competing financial interests.

Not a protocol change. If you rely on a single body-language read of stress, this is one small reason to pair it with other information. It says nothing about shelter dogs.

Descriptive interview studyn = 90U.S. veterans with shelter-adopted dogsJ Psychosoc Nurs Ment Health Serv · Oct 19Abstract only

Veterans with a mental health diagnosis who adopted “trained companion dogs” from shelters reported perceived benefits

“telephone interviews with 90 veterans who adopted dogs and had a mental health diagnosis at the time of adoption”

Muni-Sathoff et al. report that ownership influenced perceptions of loneliness (97%), sense of purpose (87%), exercise (86%), safety (86%), anxiety (84%), depression (84%), and sleep (74%).

These are self-reported perceptions from interviews, with no comparison group or before/after measure shown in the abstract. The abstract refers to “trained companion dogs (TCDs) from animal shelters” but doesn't say how participants were recruited or how “trained” was defined. No conflict-of-interest statement is shown in the record.

Could inform how you talk with veteran adopters, but 90 self-reports can't tell you whether the dogs caused the changes.

San Diego

Two items on the Santee move and one headline-only SDHS item. No ordinances, disease outbreaks, or trainer events turned up in October.

County's own releaseOct 20

County confirms Santee shelter is open and Bonita sheltering is closed for good

No. 0 and No. 1 relayed the planned Oct 15 opening. The County's release now says the $37M shelter at 452 Riverview Parkway opened Oct 15, Bonita sheltering is permanently closed, and Carlsbad continues. It lists 85 dog kennels, eight activity yards, and isolation and quarantine rooms.

Dr. Brie Sarvis, director of County Animal Services, is quoted:

“The improved housing, play yards and walking trails will make a huge difference in their daily lives.”

This is the County's own press release, so the cost and kennel counts are self-reported. KPBS (below) gives a different kennel count.

Bonita sheltering is closed and Santee is open. Ask what the yards and trails mean for your access to behavior work.

countynewscenter.com

KPBSOct 16

KPBS: more than 500 animals moved; kennel count falls from 150+ at Bonita

KPBS reports more than 500 animals were moved. It says the old Bonita site had 150+ dog kennels and the new site has 83, and describes the Santee kennels as divided so animals can move to the other side while staff clean. KPBS and the County agree on an Oct 15 opening.

“The new Santee kennels will allow us to have the dogs and cats go over to the other side of their house”

KPBS says 83 dog kennels and the County says 85; I can't resolve the difference. The article also references an earlier KPBS investigation of Bonita cleaning practices, which I did not read.

The dog kennel count appears to drop by roughly half. The articles don't say what that does to capacity, so don't assume.

SDHS own releaseOct 28Headline/summary only

SDHS discounts adult dog adoptions Oct 29–31

I relied only on the headline and summary; the page body and PDF did not load for the research subagent. The title says kennel space “reaches critical levels.” The summary says 50% off adoption fees for adult dogs ages 7 months and older, Oct 29–31.

This is the organization's own release and it is an adoption appeal. No capacity figures are used here because I couldn't read them on the page.

Adopters arriving from a discount may need the same expectation-setting as any other.
